# Lumi Business Plan

## 1. Executive Summary

Lumi is a Finnish-first social-emotional learning (SEL) companion for early childhood education. It helps kindergarten-aged children practice emotional awareness, calming, safe choices, and help-seeking through short, guided, teacher-led scenarios. The current product already includes Finnish voice interaction, structured classroom scenarios, child-friendly emotional check-ins, and privacy-conscious group-level reporting for pilots and research.

Lumi is positioned as a practical classroom tool, not just a character or content library. Its core value is helping teachers deliver consistent, repeatable, developmentally appropriate SEL moments in 6-12 minute sessions without increasing administrative burden. For schools and municipalities, Lumi offers a way to support child wellbeing, classroom climate, and early prevention while preserving privacy and enabling outcome measurement.

The initial business goal is to establish Lumi as a trusted pilot solution for Finnish kindergartens and early years programs, then expand through municipal procurement, private early education chains, and research partnerships. Over time, Lumi can grow into a broader early childhood wellbeing platform with multilingual support, richer analytics, and home-school continuity.

## 2. Mission

Lumi helps young children build emotional safety, self-regulation, and prosocial habits through warm, structured, classroom-friendly interactions.

## 3. Vision

To become the most trusted SEL companion for early childhood education in the Nordics: simple for teachers, engaging for children, safe for institutions, and credible for researchers and decision-makers.

## 4. Problem

Early childhood educators face three persistent challenges:

- Children need repeated practice with emotional regulation, boundaries, empathy, and safety skills.
- Teachers have limited time and energy to deliver high-quality SEL moments consistently.
- Schools and municipalities want evidence of impact, but most classroom wellbeing work is difficult to measure without creating privacy or documentation burdens.

Existing alternatives often fall into one of four weak categories:

- Static printed materials with low engagement.
- General edtech tools not designed for ages 4-6.
- Therapeutic or home-use apps that do not fit classroom workflows.
- Entertainment-first character products that lack reporting, educator control, or research usefulness.

Lumi addresses the gap between pedagogy, usability, and measurable implementation.

## 5. Solution

Lumi delivers short, guided SEL sessions for early childhood settings through a friendly animated companion and teacher-controlled flow. The current product direction supports:

- Guided Finnish-language scenarios on themes like hitting, throwing, mean words, turn-taking, secrets, fear, and asking adults for help.
- Teacher-led classroom use for small groups or whole-group facilitation.
- Solo or semi-personalized flows for more individualized support.
- Voice interaction and mode-based delivery, including listening, calming, firm boundary-setting, and warm reinforcement.
- Emotional check-ins before and after sessions.
- Group-level insights and session reporting without storing personal child data.

This makes Lumi valuable across three layers:

- Child value: engaging, safe, and easy-to-understand practice.
- Teacher value: ready-to-run sessions with low prep and clear facilitation.
- Institutional value: repeatable implementation and measurable group-level outcomes.

## 6. Product Scope

### Current Core Product

- SEL scenario runner for ages 4-6.
- Finnish voice and TTS support.
- Classroom and solo session modes.
- Teacher summaries and admin reporting.
- Privacy-conscious architecture based on group-level observations.

### Next Product Releases

- Expanded scenario library covering friendship, transitions, frustration, inclusion, and daily routines.
- Better teacher setup flows and onboarding.
- Pilot dashboards showing usage, emotional shift trends, and session completion.
- Localization for Swedish and English.
- Parent or caregiver takeaway summaries where appropriate and compliant.

### Long-Term Platform Opportunities

- Municipality-wide deployment dashboards.
- Research mode with configurable evaluation frameworks.
- Personalized educator recommendations based on class patterns.
- Printable and hybrid offline materials linked to Lumi sessions.
- Home extension products for optional family use.

## 7. Target Customers

### Primary Customers

- Finnish kindergartens and early childhood education centers.
- Municipal education departments responsible for wellbeing, inclusion, or innovation pilots.
- Private daycare and preschool chains seeking structured wellbeing programs.

### Secondary Customers

- Universities and research groups studying early intervention, SEL, child wellbeing, or digital pedagogy.
- NGOs and public-sector child wellbeing initiatives.
- International early years programs after Nordic validation.

### End Users

- Children ages 4-6.
- Early childhood teachers, assistants, and special education staff.
- Administrators who need implementation visibility and evidence.

## 8. Market Entry Positioning

Lumi should be positioned as:

**A teacher-guided SEL and classroom wellbeing tool for early childhood education.**

This is stronger than positioning Lumi as:

- a kids AI app
- a talking character
- a therapy product
- a generic edtech platform

The strongest wedge is practical classroom utility plus light-touch evidence generation.

## 9. Competitive Advantage

Lumi’s differentiation comes from the combination of:

- Early childhood specificity: designed for ages 4-6, not older learners.
- Classroom fit: short guided sessions built around teacher facilitation.
- Safety-oriented content: not open-ended entertainment, but structured SEL and boundary/safety practice.
- Finnish-first language and cultural fit.
- Privacy-conscious reporting: group-level insights rather than child profiling.
- Character-led engagement: warm enough for children, controlled enough for educators.
- Research readiness: repeatable scenarios and session summaries suited to pilot evaluation.

## 10. Business Model

Lumi should use a B2B and B2G model first, with optional research and services revenue.

### Revenue Streams

1. Annual school or center subscription
- Per site license for a defined number of classrooms or educators.
- Best for private providers and individual centers.

2. Municipality or district license
- Multi-site annual agreement for public early years programs.
- Includes admin dashboard, implementation support, and reporting.

3. Paid pilot programs
- 8-12 week pilot packages including onboarding, training, support, and end-of-pilot reporting.
- Strong fit for first revenue and case-study generation.

4. Research partnerships
- Paid or co-funded deployments with universities, municipalities, or foundations.
- Useful for validation, publishing, and product credibility.

5. Professional services
- Teacher training, implementation design, and custom reporting.

### Suggested Packaging

**Pilot**
- Time-bound deployment for 1-5 sites.
- Includes setup, onboarding, support, and end-of-pilot summary.

**Core**
- Annual license for everyday classroom use.
- Includes scenario library, teacher controls, and standard reporting.

**Municipal / Enterprise**
- Multi-site deployment, admin insights, rollout support, and custom success reviews.

## 11. Go-To-Market Strategy

### Phase 1: Pilot Validation

Objectives:

- Secure 3-5 high-quality pilot partners.
- Demonstrate teacher usability and child engagement.
- Produce early evidence on session usage, emotional shift, and educator satisfaction.

Tactics:

- Direct outreach to innovative kindergartens and municipal early education leaders.
- Partner with researchers or advisors in child development and education.
- Offer structured pilots with onboarding and clear evaluation metrics.
- Convert pilot outcomes into case studies and testimonials.

### Phase 2: Municipal Expansion

Objectives:

- Turn pilot evidence into larger paid deployments.
- Build procurement credibility and repeatable rollout processes.

Tactics:

- Sell to municipalities through wellbeing, early education, and inclusion budgets.
- Create a concise procurement-ready package: product overview, privacy posture, pilot evidence, implementation model, and pricing.
- Use pilot champions as references.

### Phase 3: Network Effects and Regional Growth

Objectives:

- Expand through private education groups and neighboring Nordic markets.
- Strengthen brand as a trusted early childhood wellbeing solution.

Tactics:

- Add Swedish and English support.
- Publish outcomes and thought leadership.
- Build a partner ecosystem around training, research, and implementation.

## 12. Sales Strategy

Lumi is best sold consultatively, not through self-serve alone.

### Buyer Personas

- Early childhood center director: wants practical support and staff-friendly implementation.
- Municipal program lead: wants scalable wellbeing outcomes and low risk.
- Research lead: wants structured deployment and clean reporting.

### Sales Motion

- Demo focused on real classroom use.
- Pilot proposal with success criteria.
- Short implementation cycle.
- End-of-pilot review with evidence and expansion recommendation.

### Core Sales Message

Lumi helps teachers run short, meaningful SEL moments that children actually engage with, while giving schools and municipalities privacy-safe evidence that the work is happening and helping.

## 13. Product and Impact Metrics

The first KPI set should stay simple and decision-oriented.

### Product Metrics

- Weekly active classrooms.
- Sessions per classroom per week.
- Session completion rate.
- Average session duration.
- Scenario coverage by theme.

### Educator Metrics

- Teacher onboarding completion.
- Repeat usage after first week.
- Teacher satisfaction and perceived ease of use.
- Net recommendation from pilot staff.

### Child and Program Proxy Metrics

- Participation rate per session.
- Pre/post emotional check-in shift at group level.
- Frequency of calming support prompts.
- Use of repair phrases, breathing, and safety routines.

### Commercial Metrics

- Pilot-to-paid conversion rate.
- Revenue per site.
- Sales cycle length.
- Gross retention and expansion revenue.

## 17. Funding Strategy

Lumi is well suited to a blended funding path:

- Founder-led bootstrapping for initial product development.
- Paid pilots for early non-dilutive revenue.
- Grants or innovation funding related to education, wellbeing, or early intervention.
- Strategic angel or pre-seed capital once pilot evidence is strong.

The strongest fundraising narrative will be:

- urgent child wellbeing need
- clear classroom use case
- strong product differentiation
- privacy-aware implementation
- early institutional traction

## 18. Risks and Mitigation

### Risk: Slow institutional sales cycles
Mitigation: use paid pilots as the main entry point and keep implementation lightweight.

### Risk: Product seen as nice-to-have rather than essential
Mitigation: tie Lumi to teacher pain relief, classroom climate, and wellbeing goals with measurable evidence.

### Risk: Weak evidence of impact
Mitigation: define pilot success metrics upfront and partner with credible educators or researchers.

### Risk: Overbuilding before customer validation
Mitigation: prioritize pilot readiness, reliability, and reporting over broad feature expansion.

### Risk: Privacy or trust concerns
Mitigation: keep data minimal, group-based, and transparent; produce simple documentation for buyers.

## 19. Why Lumi Can Win

Lumi does not need to become a general AI education platform to become valuable. Its winning path is focus:

- one age group
- one clear use case
- one strong regional beachhead
- one high-trust product experience for teachers and children

If Lumi becomes the easiest way for early childhood educators to run meaningful, repeatable SEL sessions with credible reporting, it can build a durable position in an important and underserved category.

## 20. Immediate Strategic Priorities

1. Make the current product unmistakably pilot-ready.
2. Turn the product story into a buyer story for schools and municipalities.
3. Secure 3-5 pilot partners with explicit success metrics.
4. Produce one excellent case study before expanding broadly.
5. Focus on trust, evidence, and classroom usefulness over feature volume.
